Model, data and caveats

Demographic engine

A one-year cohort-component projection ages 101 cohorts forward annually. Births use an age-specific fertility curve scaled to the chosen TFR; survival is calibrated to the life-expectancy assumption; net migration uses a young-adult-heavy age profile. This is the same broad class of accounting framework used by statistical agencies, simplified for exploration.

Replacement fertility is not universally 2.1. It varies with mortality and sex ratios. The 2.1 marker is an intuitive low-mortality-country benchmark.

Economic engine

Each real country is anchored to World Bank 2025 GDP in both market US dollars and purchasing-power-parity international dollars. The projection then applies the change in effective workers and the explicit output-per-worker growth assumption. PPP is the default for long-run cross-country comparisons; the market-USD view is useful for familiar current economic rankings but does not attempt to forecast exchange rates.

Workers are estimated from an age-specific participation schedule. The retirement-age control shifts older-worker participation. GDP per person divides projected output by projected population. These are scenario mechanics, not official GDP forecasts.

Preset data

The country library now includes 22 real countries plus the synthetic 10m model. The international presets use UN World Population Prospects 2024-derived 2026 population, fertility, life-expectancy and migration indicators, with broad age shares calibrated from youth and old-age dependency ratios. Australia and Japan retain the more detailed official profiles used in the first prototype.

To keep this static prototype tiny, most countries use a generated five-year starting age profile constrained to those broad shares rather than bundling the full UN single-year-age dataset. Presets are starting points, not official national forecasts. Fertility can now either remain constant or move linearly toward a user-set 2100 TFR; migration remains a constant-rate counterfactual.
